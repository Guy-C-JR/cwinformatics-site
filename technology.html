Technology/Principles

Principles we build to, stated plainly.

We don’t publish our mechanisms, decision rules, or internal architecture. We do publish the principles they are held to, because those are what a customer can reasonably expect from us and check us against.

  • 01

    Context awareness

    A request is not a bag of tokens. It has structure, history, and parts the task depends on. Our systems are designed to treat the context a task needs as something to preserve, and to recognize when they cannot tell the difference.

    In practiceUncertainty about what a task depends on is a reason to leave a request alone, not a reason to guess.

  • 02

    Selective optimization

    Optimization is applied to specific requests under specific conditions, not to everything that passes through. The default for an unfamiliar or ambiguous request is no change.

    In practiceWhether an optimization is appropriate is evaluated per request, and “not appropriate” is a normal, expected answer.

  • 03

    Observability

    What a system does to a request should be visible to the people who own the workflow: what was measured, what was changed, what was left alone, and what it cost. Observation is available before any change is enabled.

    In practiceAn observe-only mode exists so that visibility never depends on first agreeing to a change.

  • 04

    Conservative decision-making

    When efficiency and task quality pull in different directions, quality wins. When evidence is thin, nothing changes. We would rather miss an opportunity than take one we can’t justify.

    In practiceChanges are scoped, reversible, and governed by policies the customer sets, with untouched workflows as a first-class option.

  • 05

    Measured evaluation

    Every claim about an optimization is a comparison against a baseline, on the customer’s own workload, accounting for both efficiency and task quality. Caching effects are recorded separately so they are not mistaken for the change itself.

    In practiceResults are reported after measurement, scoped to the workflow they were measured on, and not generalized beyond it.

  • 06

    Ongoing evaluation

    Our own performance evaluation is continuing. We describe what the technology is designed to do and how it is held accountable, and we don’t present outcomes that haven’t been validated as if they had been.

    In practiceIf you ask us for numbers, we will tell you what has been measured, on what, and what has not.

What we say and don’t say

Credibility comes from the boundaries, too.

A few things we are careful about when describing the technology.

No proprietary detail

We describe principles and process. Algorithms, decision rules, thresholds, and internal architecture are not published here.

No universal numbers

We don’t publish savings, accuracy, or speed figures as general claims. Results depend on workload, model behavior, and caching, and are measured per workflow.

No guarantees we haven’t earned

We don’t make blanket promises about compatibility, security, or reliability. Where those matter to your workflow, they are part of the conversation and the evaluation, not a line on a website.

Bring us a workflow, not a hypothetical.

The principles above are easiest to judge against something real. Tell us how your team uses AI and we’ll talk through what we would look at first.
