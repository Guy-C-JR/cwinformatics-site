How it works/Four steps

Four steps, in order, every time.

The approach is deliberately plain. Understand the workflow, establish a baseline, evaluate opportunities, measure outcomes. Each step produces something the next one needs, and nothing is applied before it has been measured against the state it replaces.

01Understand

Understand the workflow

Before anything is measured or changed, we learn how a workflow is put together: how requests are assembled, what they carry, how often they run, and what a good result means to the people relying on it.

Purpose

Build a shared, concrete picture of the workflow so later decisions are grounded in how it really behaves.

What we look at

  • How requests are constructed
  • Where context comes from and how often it repeats
  • What success looks like for the task

What you get

A description of the workflow we both agree on, including the parts that should never be touched.

02Baseline

Establish a baseline

A baseline is a record of how the workflow behaves today, taken before any change. Without one, any later claim of improvement is just an impression.

Purpose

Create the reference point every later comparison is made against.

What we look at

  • Processing and usage per request
  • Task quality on representative cases
  • Cost signals, with caching effects noted separately

What you get

A baseline you can inspect, question, and keep, whether or not anything changes afterwards.

03Evaluate

Evaluate opportunities

With a baseline in place, we examine where information could be handled more efficiently and whether doing so would preserve the context the task depends on. The output of this step is a judgement, not a change.

Purpose

Decide which requests are candidates, which are not, and why.

What we look at

  • Which context the task actually depends on
  • Whether repeated material is being treated as new
  • Whether a candidate change is appropriate for this request at all

What you get

A short, specific list of candidates and an equally specific list of requests to leave alone.

04Measure

Measure outcomes

Candidates are applied under policies you control and compared against the baseline on efficiency and task quality together. What holds up can be extended. What doesn’t is rolled back, and the record of why is kept.

Purpose

Replace expectation with observation before any broader deployment.

What we look at

  • Efficiency relative to the baseline
  • Task quality on the cases that matter
  • How much of any difference is caching rather than the change

What you get

A decision per workflow, supported by measurement from your own workload, and a cycle that can run again as things change.

What we deliberately don’t do

Restraint is part of the method.

  • No blanket optimization

    We do not apply one treatment to every request. Workloads differ, and so does the right response to them.

  • No silent changes

    Nothing is altered in a workflow without a policy that says so and a baseline to compare against.

  • No claims ahead of measurement

    We describe what the work is designed to do. Outcomes are reported after they are measured on your workload, not before.

  • No mechanism as a substitute for evidence

    We explain principles and process. The proof that matters is the comparison against your own baseline.

Common questions

Things people ask early.

  • Does every workload benefit from this?

    No, and we would rather say so up front. Some requests need every piece of context they carry, and some workflows are too small or too sensitive to be worth changing. The evaluation step exists to find that out before anything is applied.

  • Will this change what our models see?

    Only where a policy you control says it should, and only after a baseline exists to compare against. In the observe setting, nothing about a request is altered; it is measured. Workflows can also be excluded entirely.

  • What do you need from us to begin?

    A description of how your team uses AI, which workflows you care about most, and what you are hoping to improve. The first conversation is about understanding, not integration.

  • How do you handle caching when comparing results?

    Provider caching can change what a request costs without changing what it contains. We record cache effects separately from the change under evaluation so that a comparison reflects the change itself.

  • Can you share results from other customers?

    We don’t publish customer results or general savings figures, and our own evaluation is ongoing. The comparison that matters is the one run against your baseline, on your workload.
