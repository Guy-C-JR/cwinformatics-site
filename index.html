02The customer problem

Adoption grows faster than understanding.

As AI becomes part of everyday work, usage climbs, the same information travels again and again, and workflows chain together in ways nobody planned. The invoice goes up. The explanation usually doesn’t.

Not every workload benefits from optimization. Some requests need every piece of context they carry. Part of the work is telling the difference, and leaving the rest alone.
  1. 01

    The same material, sent hundreds of times

    A policy document, a product catalogue, or a long instruction set is attached to request after request because that is the easiest way to be safe.

  2. 02

    Conversations that carry everything that came before

    An assistant’s history grows with every turn. By the end of a long session, each reply is processing a transcript that is mostly about something else.

  3. 03

    A rising bill with no clear driver

    Finance sees the total. Engineering sees request counts. Nobody can say which workflow, which team, or which habit moved the number.

  4. 04

    A desire to optimize, and a fear of breaking things

    Teams suspect there is waste, but they can’t tell which requests are safe to change and which ones depend on every word.

03What we help customers do

Three questions, answered in order.

Visibility comes first. Opportunities come from visibility. Changes come only when there is a basis for them.

01 / 03

Understand your usage.

See how AI requests consume resources, and where a closer look may be worthwhile. Usage is broken down by workflow, request shape, and the share of each request that is repeated from one call to the next.

  • 01Which workflows generate the most processing
  • 02How much of a typical request is context the model has already seen
  • 03Where investigation is likely to pay off, and where it isn’t
02 / 03

Identify optimization opportunities.

Evaluate whether information can be handled more efficiently while keeping the context the task requires. Not every request is a candidate, and the evaluation says so.

  • 01Separate the context a task needs from the context it merely carries
  • 02Distinguish repeated material from genuinely new information
  • 03Flag requests that should be left exactly as they are
03 / 03

Apply changes with control.

Use deliberate policies and measured evaluation to decide where optimization belongs. It is something you turn on for specific workflows, under conditions you set, after you have seen the evidence.

  • 01Observe first, change later
  • 02Scope changes to the workflows that justify them
  • 03Keep fragile or sensitive workflows untouched

04Approach/Four steps

A sequence, not a switch.

Each step earns the next. Nothing changes in a workflow until there is a baseline to compare against, and nothing is extended until the comparison holds.

Read the full approach

  1. Step 01Understand

    Understand the workflow

    Learn how requests are built, what they carry, and what a good result looks like for the people who depend on it.

  2. Step 02Baseline

    Establish a baseline

    Record how the workflow behaves today, including usage, cost signals, and task quality, so any change can be judged honestly.

  3. Step 03Evaluate

    Evaluate opportunities

    Examine where information could be handled more efficiently, and where it shouldn’t be touched, before anything is applied.

  4. Step 04Measure

    Measure outcomes

    Compare against the baseline on both efficiency and quality. Keep what holds up. Roll back what doesn’t.

05Built around evidence

Efficiency only counts if the task still succeeds.

Useful optimization has to account for two things at once: how much processing a request needs, and whether the result is still right. We treat measurement as the product, not the paperwork.

  • Workload characteristics

    Long documents, short questions, and structured records behave differently. Results depend on what a workflow actually sends.

  • Model behavior

    Different models respond differently to the same change in context. What holds for one may not hold for another.

  • Caching

    Provider caching can change what a request costs without changing what it contains. Accounting has to separate the two.

06Areas of relevance

Where this kind of work tends to matter.

These are the shapes of workflow we are built to examine. They describe relevance, not a list of deployments.

  • 01

    AI assistants

    Multi-turn sessions where history accumulates and each reply carries the whole conversation with it.

  • 02

    Document-heavy workflows

    Review, summarization, and question answering over long material that is often reused across many requests.

  • 03

    Structured data processing

    Extraction, classification, and transformation of records where the task depends on specific fields rather than everything attached.

  • 04

    Operational analysis

    Recurring reports and analyses that run on a schedule, where small inefficiencies repeat every single time.
